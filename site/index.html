Getting started

Three steps, about five minutes

Install it on a PC that stays on

A laptop, a desktop, or a cheap mini PC in a closet. The installer needs no admin rights and ticks “start with Windows” for you.

Let it find your speakers

It browses your network continuously and lists every Cast device it sees, with live volume for each. Nothing to configure.

Group them and set gains

Tick which speakers move together, nudge each room’s gain until the house sounds even, and close the window to the tray.

Devices

Sync groups that actually stay in sync

Tick the speakers you want to move together. When any member changes — from the app, the Google Home app, or a tap on the speaker — the others are set to match within a moment. Groups are yours, independent of Google’s cast groups, and each one has its own slider and media controls.

Sync Groups

Whole-house volume from the tray

You don’t need to open the window. Right-click the tray icon, pick a group, pick a level. 5% steps, plus play, pause, next and previous for whatever is casting.

Schedules for bedtime and mornings

Weekly rules with a time and day checkboxes: drop “Everything” to 50% at 10 pm on school nights, bring the kitchen back up in the morning. Each rule can target several groups or devices at different levels, and toggles on and off with one checkbox.

Schedule

Set it and forget it

Start with Windows, minimised to the tray. Optional automatic updates from GitHub releases. Export your devices, names, groups and schedules to a JSON file and import them on another PC.

Questions

The things people ask first

Does it need the internet or a Google account?

No. It speaks the Cast protocol directly to your speakers over your own network, so it keeps working if your internet drops. The only optional outbound connection is the update check, which looks at this project’s GitHub releases and is off unless you turn it on.

Does it fight with the Google Home app?

The opposite — it listens. Change a volume in the Google Home app, on your phone, or by tapping the speaker itself, and the app notices within a moment and brings the rest of that sync group to match.

What is Sync Gain for?

Rooms are not equally loud. Sync Gain is a per-device multiplier from 0–200%: a speaker set to 50% gain sits at half the group’s level, and when you change that speaker it reports back at double, so the group stays consistent. Set it once and the whole house moves together at the balance you chose.

Does it run without logging in to Windows?

Not yet. It installs as a normal startup app, so it launches when you log in and then sits in the tray. On a dedicated mini PC that is left logged in, that behaves like an always-on service. A true no-login Windows service is on the list.

Will it work with my Google cast groups?

Yes. Cast groups you made in the Google Home app are discovered and get their own section, with volume and playback control. They stay out of sync-group membership on purpose: a cast group’s volume is already an aggregate of its members, so syncing it against them would double-apply every change.

Is it really free? What’s the catch?

It is free and the source is on GitHub under an open licence. There is no telemetry and nothing to buy. If it saves your sanity, the tip jar and the Amazon links are how it stays maintained.

Did it make your house sound better?

Volume Sync is free and open source, built and maintained by one person. If it saved you from shouting “Hey Google, volume down” in three rooms, a coffee goes a long way toward keeping it that way. A Ferrari would go further, but I’ll settle for the coffee.
